Rereading your notes feels like studying. Mostly, it just makes the page look familiar.

Familiar isn't the same as remembered. The second time you read a page, your brain says "got it." Then the exam asks you to produce the answer with the page closed. That's a different skill, and rereading barely trains it.

Learning researchers have a name for the fix: retrieval practice. Pulling an answer out of your own memory strengthens it more than looking at it again. Spreading those attempts over a few days helps even more.

Try it this week. You don't need new notes:

Day 1, right after the lecture: close your notes and write down everything you can remember. Then check what you missed.

Day 3: quiz yourself only on the parts you missed.

Day 7: one mixed review across the whole topic, notes closed.

Same notes, three short sessions. It'll feel harder than rereading. Good. The effort of remembering is what makes it stick.

Tip: write the questions on day 1. Fresh lecture, better questions.
