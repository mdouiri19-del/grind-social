"It was in the syllabus."

Every student has heard that line. Usually the day after they needed to know.

Here's how it happens. In week 1 you get 5 course outlines as PDFs. You skim them, copy down the big dates, and start a to-do list.

From then on, that list is your semester. But it only holds what you remembered to write down. Lab 2. Quiz 4, moved to Friday. The 10% response paper on page 4. None of those made it onto the list, so in your head they don't exist.

By November the list feels endless, and it's still missing the item that matters most. You cross one thing off, 3 more show up, and the one buried in a PDF shows up last. Midterm 2, Thursday, 9 AM. You find out Wednesday night.

That isn't a discipline problem. It's a source problem. Your list starts from memory, when it should start from the documents that already hold every date and weight.

That's the angle we take with GRIND. You import the syllabus, check the dates and weights it pulls out, and your list starts complete. Then it shows what's due in the next 14 days, sorted by date and weight.

You still do the work. You just stop meeting deadlines by surprise.
