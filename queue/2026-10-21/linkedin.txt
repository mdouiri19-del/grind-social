A 30% paper due in 3 weeks isn't one task. It's 6.

Most students put it on their list as one item, "write the paper," and it sits there looking too big to start until about 4 days before the deadline, when it suddenly feels very real.

So here's the backwards plan we'd use. Start at the due date and count back:

Day 21: submit. Nothing new gets written that day.
Day 18: full draft done, so you still have 3 days to fix it.
Day 14: outline, with every source attached to a section.
Day 10: your thesis, in one sentence. If it won't fit, it isn't ready.
Day 7: sources chosen, not just collected.
Day 1: read the rubric and list what earns the marks.

Each step fits in one evening.

That's the whole trick. Nobody puts off "write one sentence." We all put off "write the paper," even though the work is the same size either way and only the first step looks different.

It also shows you slipping early. Miss the Day 14 outline and you've got a small problem to fix. Find out on Day 20? Too late.
