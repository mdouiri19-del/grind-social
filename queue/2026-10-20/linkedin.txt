Most missed deadlines aren't a discipline problem. They're a visibility problem.

Here's how it usually goes.

Every course hands out its own syllabus in September. Each one makes sense on its own. A 10% lab report in Biology. A problem set in Economics. A 20% essay draft in English, due in week 7.

Nobody reads them side by side.

So a student checks one course at a time, and every date looks far away. Then Thursday shows up. Four things are due the same night, all at 11:59 PM, in 4 different course portals.

They saw every one of those dates. They just never saw them together.

The usual advice is "get a planner" or "start earlier." Both assume you already know what's coming. Most of the time you don't, because the dates are spread across five PDFs written by five people who never compared notes.

That's the problem we built GRIND. around. You upload each syllabus, and every date and weight lands in one list, sorted, with the next 14 days up front. The collision shows up on Monday instead of Thursday night. It's free for students, link on our page.

Starting early is easy once you can see what to start.

If you teach: have you ever lined up your deadlines next to the other courses your students take that term?
