Same 3 hours of studying. Spread over a week, they stick. Crammed into one night, they fade fast.

Memory researchers call this the spacing effect, and it's one of the most tested results in learning science.

When you come back to a topic after 2 or 3 days, you've lost a bit of it. Pulling it back out takes effort, and that's the part that makes it last.

Cramming skips that step. At 1 AM everything feels familiar because you read it 10 minutes ago. But you'd struggle to answer a question on it cold.

A simple plan for your next midterm:

Start 14 days out. Pick 3 or 4 evenings, about an hour each, with a couple of days between them.

Open each session with your notes closed. Write out the main ideas from memory, or do 5 practice questions. Then open the notes and fix what you missed.

Save the last evening for whatever you've missed twice.

The total time doesn't change. You're just moving it earlier.
