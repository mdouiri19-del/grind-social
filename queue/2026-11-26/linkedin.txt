A 30% paper due in 2 weeks sits on your list as 1 line. So no evening ever feels like the right one to start it.

This week's build note is about that line.

In GRIND., your deadlines come from your syllabus, sorted by date with the weight next to each one. A 5% quiz and a 30% paper take the same space on that list. The quiz takes 1 evening. The paper doesn't. It takes 2 weeks of small pieces.

So Study Studio makes assignment plans. First it reads your own course files, like the brief, and splits the paper into steps: read the brief, pick a question, find sources, outline, draft, edit.

Each step gets its own date before the deadline, and you tick it off when it's done.

Now on a Tuesday night, the list doesn't say "final paper." It says "find sources, due Saturday." That fits in 1 evening, so you start.

We built it for the end of term, when papers and finals land in the same 2 weeks and every big one looks too big to open.

Students: which step of a paper do you always leave for the last night?
